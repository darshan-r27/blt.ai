# Telugu style notes

Rules for drafting the Telugu course (for the Tamil speaker). **Nothing here has been reviewed yet.** Level 1 was
drafted by Claude on 2026-10-12 from the choices below; the Telugu reviewer should correct every choice that is
wrong, and each correction becomes a rule in this file before Level 2 is drafted
(`docs/COURSE_SYLLABUS.md` section 7). This document holds no Telugu: examples are cited by item id, so open the
lesson file (or the editor) to see them.

## Choices the drafter made (please confirm or correct)

- **Variety:** standard spoken Telugu of Coastal Andhra, as heard in films and television
  (DECISIONS 042). Where a common word differs by region, the Coastal Andhra word is the answer and the other
  common forms are accepted spellings.
- **Two registers:** casual (the informal "you", short verb endings) and respectful (the polite "you", polite
  verb endings and the polite particle). `neutral` means no you-form.
- **Address words:** the male-friend particle for casual talk to a man, and the female-friend particle for casual
  talk to a woman (see te-l01-u01-i01, i03 and i12). Please confirm that these are what friends really use,
  and tell us if one particle is normally used for both.
- **"You" plural and respect:** respectful sentences use the polite "you" and the polite ending together. The
  `registerVariant` of a respectful sentence is its casual twin and vice versa.
- **Loanwords:** everyday English words stay English (bus, ticket, bill, card, change, phone, doctor, rest, time,
  office, bank and similar), with the Telugu light verb ("do", "give", "put") around them.
- **Romanisation:** informal Latin spelling as in Telugu WhatsApp chats. Long vowels are written doubled in the
  answer where it helps reading; accepted spellings carry the single-vowel and doubled-consonant variants and
  the with/without question mark variants.
- **Numbers and days:** the everyday spoken numbers and the usual Telugu day names (see te-l01-u06 and
  te-l01-u07), with the English day name accepted where speakers use it.
- **Same rules as Tamil** for accepted spellings (3 to 6), wrong options (real sentences with a different meaning;
  3 for neutral items, 2 plus the variant otherwise), glosses (`tokens`), and `script` (Telugu letters only, English
  loanwords spelled in Telugu letters; no Telugu letters anywhere else).
